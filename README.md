## The pitch

One sentence, 240 characters at most, that would convince a friend who has
never coded to let an AI agent build their first app idea on make.fun:

> Tell the app idea you've been carrying around in your head for months to an AI agent on make.fun in plain words tonight, and by tomorrow you could be tapping through a real first version — no code, no hiring, no waiting.

220 characters, one sentence.

## How it works

`main.py` (Python 3.13, standard library only) writes the pitch to
`/output/entry.txt`. Nothing else is needed: no network, no dependencies,
no configuration.

```sh
python3 main.py
cat /output/entry.txt
```

## Why this line

Ten candidates were drafted and weighed against the judge's brief, in the
brief's own order:

1. **Makes the case to that friend.** It speaks to the friend's own
   situation — an idea they have been carrying around for months — instead
   of pitching a market.
2. **Persuasion, now.** "Tonight" and "by tomorrow" give a reason to say
   yes today, and the triple "no code, no hiring, no waiting" makes handing
   the idea over feel easy and low risk.
3. **Clarity.** Plain words, one read, no jargon.
4. **Truth.** "Could be" hedges the timing honestly; it promises a first
   version to try, not a finished app, and claims nothing about cost.
5. **Warmth and craft.** "Carrying around in your head for months" and
   "tapping through a real first version" are a fresh turn of phrase, not
   a template or a slogan.
